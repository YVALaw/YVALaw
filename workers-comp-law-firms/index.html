Why language matters in workers' comp

Workers' comp clients are often hurt, out of work and worried about money. Many work in jobs where Spanish is their first language. When they call a firm and cannot explain what happened in the language they think in, details get lost: how the injury happened, when it was reported, who the supervisor was, which doctor they saw.

With bilingual staff, the worker tells the story in Spanish and your attorney reads clear notes in English. The same staff member can handle every follow-up call afterwards, which builds trust over a case that may last months. Our article on bilingual staff for law firms goes into more detail.

Intake for injured workers

A YVA legal intake specialist answers calls and web leads and follows your screening script. Typical questions include:

  • Date, place and description of the injury
  • Employer name and the worker's job
  • Whether and when the injury was reported to the employer
  • Medical treatment so far and the treating provider
  • Current work status and any paperwork the worker has received

The specialist enters everything in your intake form and CRM, books the consultation and follows up on missing documents. Your attorney decides whether the firm takes the case.

Medical appointment and treatment follow-up

Treatment drives a workers' comp case, and keeping track of it is constant work. A YVA case manager can:

  • Check in with clients before and after medical appointments
  • Confirm visits and treatment status with providers
  • Request medical records and reports, and track what is outstanding
  • Note missed appointments or gaps in treatment for the attorney
  • Keep the client informed in English or Spanish

Forms and deadlines

Workers' comp involves state-specific forms, filings and hearing dates. Your staff do not decide which forms apply or which deadlines govern a case; your attorneys do. What staff can do:

  • Prepare forms from your templates for attorney review
  • Enter the deadlines and hearing dates your attorneys set into your calendar or case system
  • Send reminders to attorneys and clients ahead of each date
  • Keep the case file organized so documents are ready when needed

No legal or medical advice

YVA staff do not advise workers on their claim, their benefits or their medical care. They gather information, coordinate and keep files moving under your attorneys' supervision. Your attorneys remain responsible for the legal work, and no staffing service can guarantee case outcomes.

Frequently asked questions

Can virtual staff handle intake for workers' comp cases?

Yes. A YVA intake specialist answers calls in English or Spanish, follows your screening script, enters the information in your CRM and books consultations. The attorney decides whether to accept each case.

Do your staff speak Spanish?

Yes. Every YVA staff member is fully bilingual in English and Spanish, so injured workers can be served in their preferred language from intake through case updates.

Can a virtual case manager follow up on medical treatment?

Yes. Case managers check in with clients about appointments, confirm treatment with providers, request records and report gaps in care to the attorney. They do not give medical or legal advice.

Is it allowed to use outsourced staff at a workers' comp firm?

Generally, yes, when attorneys supervise the work and protect client confidentiality. ABA Model Rule 5.3 (supervising nonlawyer assistants) and Rule 1.6 (confidentiality) are the usual references; check your state bar's guidance.
