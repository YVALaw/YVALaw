**Post 1 — Tuesday, Dec 1 — Tip: write the intake script down** (896 characters)

```text
The fastest way to improve your firm's intake has nothing to do with hiring: write the script down.

In many firms, intake lives in one person's head. When that person is out, busy or leaves, every caller gets a different conversation, and the attorney gets a different set of facts.

A good intake script fits on one page per case type:
1. The questions you need answered on every call, in order.
2. Your eligibility rules: case types, locations and dates you take.
3. Approved answers to "Do I have a case?" and "What does it cost?" (the answer is a consultation, not legal advice).
4. When to escalate, and to whom.
5. What goes into the CRM before the call ends.

Write it in English and Spanish if you serve Spanish-speaking clients. Then anyone who answers the phone, in the office or remote, gives the same first impression.
```

**Post 2 — Thursday, Dec 3 — Myth vs. fact** (894 characters)

```text
Myth: virtual staff are fine for back-office work, but clients should only ever talk to people in the office.

Fact: your clients care about three things when they call. Did someone answer? Did they understand me? Did the firm follow up when it said it would?

None of those depends on where the desk is. They depend on training, a clear script, access to your systems and supervision by your attorneys.

What should stay in the office: anything that needs a person physically there, like mail, paper files and in-person filings. And what stays with the attorney no matter what: legal advice, case decisions and fee conversations.

Phones, intake, calendars, client updates and case follow-up all work remotely when the setup is right. That is the work YVA Law Staffing's bilingual assistants do every day for U.S. law firms.
```

**Post 3 — Tuesday, Dec 8 — Bilingual intake** (871 characters)

```text
If a Spanish-speaking caller reaches your firm and nobody can speak with them comfortably, you usually do not get a second chance. They call the next firm.

"We have someone who speaks some Spanish" is not the same as bilingual intake. Intake means asking about injuries, treatment, insurance and dates, and getting accurate answers from someone who is stressed and often in pain. That takes real fluency in both languages, plus the patience to explain the next steps clearly.

Three questions to check your coverage this week:
1. Who answers a Spanish-speaking call at 7 p.m.?
2. Is your intake script available in Spanish?
3. Are your follow-up texts and emails in the client's language?
```
