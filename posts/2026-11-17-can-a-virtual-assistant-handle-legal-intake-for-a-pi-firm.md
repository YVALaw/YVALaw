Yes. A trained virtual intake specialist can handle most of a personal injury firm's intake: answering calls and emails in English and Spanish, screening callers against the firm's criteria, collecting the accident facts, entering everything into the CRM, booking consultations and following up on leads that go quiet. What stays with the attorney is the legal judgment: evaluating the claim, deciding whether to accept the case and answering any legal question. The difference between intake that works and intake that loses cases is less about where the person sits and more about whether they are trained for legal intake, follow your script and are supervised.

## What personal injury intake actually involves

PI intake is a fact-gathering and speed job. A potential client has just been hurt, is often stressed and is frequently calling more than one firm. The intake person has to collect a clean, complete record in one conversation, then get the caller in front of an attorney before someone else does.

The facts your firm wants on every new matter usually include:

- When and where the accident happened, and what kind it was (car, truck, slip and fall, workplace, dog bite)
- The injuries, whether the person has seen a doctor and where they are treating now
- Insurance information for both sides, and whether there is a police or incident report
- Whether they already have a lawyer or have signed anything with an insurer
- The names of the other people involved, which your firm needs for its conflict check

None of this requires a law degree. It requires a consistent script, patience, good notes and fast follow-up. That is exactly the work a dedicated intake specialist is hired to do.

## What a virtual intake specialist can handle

A [virtual legal intake specialist](/virtual-legal-intake/) does the same work an in-office intake coordinator does, from a remote desk inside your systems:

- **Answer calls and emails** in the caller's preferred language, English or Spanish.
- **Screen and qualify** each inquiry using the questions and eligibility rules your firm sets.
- **Complete your intake form and CRM entry** in tools like Clio, MyCase, HubSpot or Salesforce, so the file is ready when the attorney opens it.
- **Book consultations** directly on your attorneys' calendars.
- **Follow up on leads** who did not book, did not send documents or stopped answering.
- **Chase paperwork** your attorneys send out, such as signed forms and medical authorizations, with reminders on a schedule.

Because the specialist does only this, the phone does not go to voicemail just because the paralegal is on another call.

## What should stay with your attorneys

Intake staff, virtual or in-house, are nonlawyers. Some lines should never move:

- **No legal advice.** The specialist does not tell a caller whether they "have a case," what it is worth or what they should say to the insurer.
- **No case acceptance.** The attorney decides which matters the firm takes.
- **Conflict decisions stay with the firm.** The specialist collects names; the firm runs the conflict check and makes the call.
- **Fee agreements are the attorney's.** The specialist can send and track paperwork; the attorney explains the terms and answers questions about them.

## How to set up virtual PI intake so it works

Remote intake fails for the same reasons in-office intake fails: no script, no rules and no feedback. Put these in place before the first shift:

1. **A written intake script** in English and Spanish, with the exact questions for each case type and approved answers to common questions like "Do I have a case?" and "How much does a lawyer cost?"
2. **Eligibility rules.** Case types you take, locations you cover, cut-off dates you care about and red flags that mean "decline politely" or "escalate now."
3. **Escalation steps.** Who gets a text or Slack message for an urgent call, such as a catastrophic injury or a caller with a deadline close by, and what to do if that person does not answer.
4. **CRM fields and calendar rules.** Which fields are required, which attorney takes which consults and how much notice they need.
5. **A daily summary.** New leads, booked consults, declined calls and anyone waiting on a decision.
6. **A weekly review.** In the first weeks, an attorney or office manager reviews a sample of intake records and gives feedback. That review is part of supervising the work.
