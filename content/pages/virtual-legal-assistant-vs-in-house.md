## Supervision, security and confidentiality

The rules don't change because the assistant is remote. Under the ABA Model Rules, a lawyer must make reasonable efforts to ensure nonlawyer assistants, including outside ones, act consistently with the lawyer's professional obligations (Rule 5.3), and must make reasonable efforts to protect client information (Rule 1.6). Most state bars follow similar rules, and many have issued guidance on outsourcing. Check your own state bar's guidance; this page is not legal advice.

In practice, treat a virtual assistant like any remote employee:

- Give each person their own login, never a shared one, with access only to what their role needs.
- Turn on two-factor authentication and keep files inside your systems (Clio, MyCase, Google Workspace) rather than on personal devices.
- Put confidentiality terms in writing.
- Keep the attorney in charge: YVA assistants don't give legal advice and work under attorney supervision. The attorney stays responsible for the legal work.

## When in-house is the better choice

Be honest about the job before you decide. Hire in-house if:

- **The work is physical.** Greeting walk-ins, sorting mail, handling paper files, filing in person at the courthouse or notarizing documents all need someone on site.
- **Your systems can't be reached remotely.** If your case files live on a local server or in paper folders, fix that first or hire locally.
- **You're building a career pipeline.** If you want to grow a paralegal or future associate inside the firm, in-person mentoring is hard to replace.
- **Nobody has time to give direction.** A virtual assistant needs clear instructions and a written process. If no one at the firm can provide that, an in-house hire will struggle too, but at least they can watch and ask.

## When virtual is the better choice

- Calls go unanswered after hours or during busy stretches.
- Spanish-speaking callers hang up or wait for the one person who speaks Spanish.
- Attorneys spend billable hours on calendars, follow-ups and file organization.
- Demand letters or case updates sit in a backlog.
- You need to add capacity now without adding salary, benefits and a desk.

A hybrid often works well: one in-house person for the front office and virtual staff for phones, intake, demand writing or case management. See [virtual legal assistants](/virtual-legal-assistants/) for what a YVA assistant handles day to day.
