## The hidden costs most firms don't budget

- **Hiring time.** Writing the post, reading applications, interviews and reference checks take attorney or office-manager hours that could have been billed.
- **Turnover.** Every time someone leaves, you pay the hiring cost again, plus the weeks when calls go unanswered.
- **Training.** A person new to legal work needs to learn intake questions, conflict checks, deadlines and your case management system before they save you any time.
- **Supervision.** Someone at the firm has to direct and review the work, whichever option you choose. Plan for it.

## Part-time vs. full-time

Because billing is hourly, cost scales with the hours you use. A full-time month is {hours} hours. If your volume only justifies part of a schedule, ask on the call what a reduced schedule would look like; the math is simply the hourly rate times the hours.

Two things to keep in mind:

- **Continuity matters.** One person who knows your cases and your clients works faster than several people sharing the load.
- **Round-the-clock coverage takes more than one person.** A week has 168 hours and one full-time person covers about 40 of them. If you want calls answered nights and weekends, price it as several schedules.

## How to estimate what you need

1. **Track one normal week.** Count missed calls, new inquiries, hours attorneys spend on admin, and demands or follow-ups waiting in the backlog.
2. **Sort the work by role.** Phones and screening go to intake; calendar and correspondence to a legal assistant; medical records and demand letters to a demand writer; keeping cases moving to a case manager.
3. **Count hours per role.** About 40 hours a week is one full-time person.
4. **Multiply hours by the role's rate.** Use the table above.
5. **Compare.** Set the total against the in-house figure and against the attorney hours you get back.
