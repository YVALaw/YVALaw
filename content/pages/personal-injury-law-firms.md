## Where PI firms get stretched

Personal injury practices run on volume and follow-through. The same pressure points come up again and again:

- **Calls after hours.** Accidents happen at any hour, and injured people call when they can. A call that goes to voicemail may go to another firm.
- **Spanish-speaking clients.** Many PI clients are more comfortable in Spanish. If only one person in the office speaks it, that person becomes a bottleneck.
- **Medical records.** Every case depends on records and bills from multiple providers, and chasing them takes hours.
- **Demand backlog.** Cases finish treatment faster than demands get written.
- **Status calls.** Clients want updates, and attorneys end up answering them.

## How PI firms typically combine roles

Most firms do not hire every role at once. Common combinations, depending on where the bottleneck is:

1. **Intake first.** For firms losing leads to missed calls or language barriers. One bilingual intake specialist answers, screens and books.
2. **Intake + demand writer.** For firms signing cases but falling behind on demands. Intake keeps new cases coming; the writer clears the demand queue.
3. **Intake + demand writer + case manager.** The full pipeline. Intake signs the client, the case manager keeps treatment and records moving, and the demand writer receives complete files ready for drafting.

Some firms add a legal assistant to support attorneys with calendars and correspondence alongside any of these.

## How a case moves through a virtual PI team

1. **Intake** answers the call in English or Spanish, collects the accident details your firm asks for, enters the lead in your CRM and books the consultation or sends the sign-up paperwork.
2. **The case manager** makes the welcome call, checks in on treatment, coordinates with providers and requests records and bills as treatment progresses.
3. **The demand writer** receives the complete file, reviews the medical records, calculates damages and drafts the demand to your firm's standards.
4. **Your attorney** reviews and approves the demand, and leads negotiation. The case manager keeps the client updated throughout.
