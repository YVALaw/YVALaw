## What a virtual case manager owns

A legal assistant supports attorneys task by task. A case manager is responsible for a set of cases, making sure each one keeps moving. In practice that means:

- **Coordinating everyone on the case.** The case manager is the point person between the client, the attorney, medical providers and insurance adjusters.
- **Treatment follow-up.** Checking in with clients about appointments, confirming treatment with providers and noting gaps in care for the attorney.
- **Records and bills.** Requesting and tracking medical records and bills so files are complete when they are needed.
- **Deadline tracking.** Keeping the dates your attorneys set visible and acting before they arrive.
- **Status reporting.** Regular updates for attorneys and proactive updates for clients, so fewer clients call asking "what is happening with my case?"

## PI case management, step by step

Personal injury is where most firms first feel the need for a case manager. A typical path through a case:

1. **Sign-up handoff.** The new client comes over from intake. The case manager makes the welcome call and explains what happens next.
2. **Treatment phase.** Regular client check-ins, provider coordination and notes on treatment status.
3. **Records collection.** Requesting records and bills from every provider and tracking what is still outstanding.
4. **Demand handoff.** When treatment ends and the file is complete, the case moves to the [demand writer](/demand-writing-services/) or attorney.
5. **Negotiation and resolution.** Ongoing status updates for the client and attorney while the attorney negotiates.

- Clients call regularly asking for updates.
- Attorneys are chasing records and providers themselves.
- Cases stall after sign-up because no one is checking on treatment.
- Deadlines are tracked in someone's head or inbox.
- You have Spanish-speaking clients and status calls depend on one bilingual staff member.
