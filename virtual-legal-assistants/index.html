What a virtual legal assistant does

A legal assistant keeps the practice running so your attorneys spend their hours on legal work. The core responsibilities:

  • Calendar management. Scheduling client meetings, consultations, depositions and calls; confirming appointments; keeping each attorney's calendar accurate.
  • Client correspondence. Answering and sending routine emails and calls, sending document requests and status updates, in English or Spanish.
  • Case file organization. Naming, filing and indexing documents in your case management system so anyone can find what they need.
  • Deadline tracking. Entering the dates your attorneys set, sending reminders and flagging anything coming up.
  • Document drafting. Preparing letters, forms and first drafts from your templates for attorney review.

What a virtual legal assistant does not do

Support, not legal advice

Assistants do not give legal advice, sign filings or make strategy decisions. They work under attorney supervision, and the attorney stays responsible for the legal work and its ethics. Drafts go to an attorney for review before they leave the firm.

A typical week (example)

Every firm is different, but a full-time assistant's week often looks like this:

Day Typical focus
Monday Review the week's calendar, confirm appointments, list deadlines due in the next 14 days
Tuesday Client correspondence, document requests, file new records into the case system
Wednesday Draft letters and forms from templates, send to attorney for review
Thursday Chase outstanding documents, update case notes, prepare files for upcoming meetings
Friday Clean up the file system, send the attorney a short status summary, plan next week

The schedule bends around your attorneys. Some firms use their assistant mostly for scheduling and client contact; others lean on drafting and file work.

Who it is for

A virtual legal assistant tends to make the most sense when:

  • An attorney is spending evenings on email, scheduling and filing instead of billable or case work.
  • The firm has Spanish-speaking clients and no one on staff who can speak with them comfortably.
  • You need help now and cannot wait through a long local hiring process.
  • You want a full-time person without the full cost of an in-house hire.

If your main bottleneck is the phone and new leads, a virtual legal intake specialist is the better first hire. If you need someone to own each case from sign-up to resolution, look at virtual case managers.

Frequently asked questions

What is a virtual legal assistant?

A virtual legal assistant is a remote staff member who supports attorneys with calendars, client correspondence, case files, deadlines and document drafting. They work under attorney supervision and do not give legal advice.

How much does a virtual legal assistant cost?

YVA Law Staffing charges $9.50 an hour for a legal assistant. Full-time, at 160 hours a month, that is about $1,520 a month.

Can a virtual legal assistant use Clio or MyCase?

Yes. YVA assistants work inside your existing tools, including Clio, MyCase, HubSpot, Salesforce, Slack and Google Workspace.

Is it ethical for a law firm to use a virtual assistant?

Generally, yes, when the attorney supervises the assistant and protects client confidentiality. ABA Model Rules 5.3 (supervising nonlawyer assistants) and 1.6 (confidentiality) are the usual references; check your state bar's guidance.

Do I have to sign a long-term contract?

No. There is a service agreement but no long-term lock-in, and you can exit at any time without penalties.
