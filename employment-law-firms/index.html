Intake screening for employment inquiries

Employment firms hear from many people who are not sure whether they have a claim. Good intake separates the inquiries your attorneys should review quickly from the ones that do not fit your practice, without the attorney spending an hour on each call.

A YVA legal intake specialist follows your screening script for each inquiry type:

  • Wage and hour. Employer name, job title, dates of employment, how the worker was paid, hours worked, and whether overtime or breaks are in question.
  • Discrimination and harassment. What happened, when, who was involved, whether it was reported internally, and whether the person is still employed.
  • Wrongful termination. Termination date, the reason given, events leading up to it, and any paperwork received.

The specialist records the answers in your intake form and CRM, notes the dates your attorneys will want to check, and books a consultation for the inquiries that meet your criteria. The attorney decides whether the firm takes the matter.

Facts, not legal opinions

Intake staff do not tell callers whether they have a case, what it is worth or what deadline applies. They collect information consistently and pass it to your attorneys, who make those calls. Time-sensitive matters can be flagged for same-day attorney review using rules your firm sets.

Serving Spanish-speaking workers

Many workers with wage and hour or workplace treatment concerns are more comfortable explaining what happened in Spanish. Details matter in these cases, and a caller who has to struggle through a second language may leave out the facts that matter most.

Every YVA staff member is fully bilingual, so Spanish-speaking callers can explain their situation in their own words, and your attorneys receive clear notes in English. The same person can handle follow-up calls, document requests and status updates in Spanish for the life of the case. For more on why this matters, see bilingual staff for law firms.

Organizing employment case documents

Employment cases are document-heavy: pay stubs, time records, schedules, offer letters, handbooks, performance reviews, termination letters, emails and text messages. Clients often send them in pieces, from their phones, over weeks.

A virtual legal assistant can:

  • Request specific documents from clients and track what is still missing
  • Name, file and index everything in your case management system
  • Build simple chronologies of dates and events for attorney review
  • Prepare letters and forms from your templates
  • Track the deadlines your attorneys set and send reminders

Confidentiality and supervision

Employment clients share sensitive information about their jobs, health and finances. Your YVA staff work inside your firm's own systems and under your attorneys' supervision. ABA Model Rule 5.3 (supervising nonlawyer assistants) and Rule 1.6 (confidentiality) are the general references for using outside staff; check your state bar's guidance for the specific rules in your jurisdiction.
